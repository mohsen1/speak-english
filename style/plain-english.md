# Plain English

Write for a reader who knows the subject but not this answer.

## Shape

- Lead with the conclusion, the change, or the operational fact.
- Say it once. Do not restate the question, and do not close with a paragraph
  that repeats what the text already said.
- Match the length to the content. An answer under about five lines is prose:
  no headings, no bullets, no bold.
- Use bullets for parallel facts, numbered lists for ordered work, and tables
  only for real comparisons or mappings. Never add structure to look thorough.
- Match the shape of the text you are given. Do not invent new structure.

## Sentences

These follow ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English.

1. One idea per sentence. Keep an instruction to 20 words or fewer, and a
   description to 25 words or fewer.
2. Six sentences per paragraph at most.
3. Use active voice and name the actor. "The migration locks the table", not
   "the table is locked". Where the text does not say who acts, keep the
   passive rather than inventing an actor.
4. Use the present tense where the content allows it.
5. Use the same word for the same thing every time. Do not vary a term for
   elegance.
6. Use no noun cluster longer than three words. "Feed background image job
   retry policy" becomes "the retry policy for feed background image jobs".
7. Keep the articles. Do not write telegram style.
8. Prefer a finite verb to an -ing form. Replace a participial lead-in
   ("Having checked X, the code...") with two sentences.
9. Write what is true, not what is not true. Remove double negatives.
10. Give an abbreviation its full form on first use, unless it is an identifier
    from the code or the product.
11. Give the number when there is one. Do not soften it into "several" or "a
    number of".

## Cut these tells

- Praise and agreement openers: "You're absolutely right", "Great question",
  "Excellent point", "Good catch".
- Filler openers: "It's worth noting that", "I should mention", "In essence",
  "Essentially", "Fundamentally", "At its core", "Simply put".
- Narration of intent: "Let me...", "I'll go ahead and...", "Now let's...".
- Transition scaffolding: "Furthermore", "Moreover", "Additionally", "In
  conclusion", "Overall", "That said", "Importantly", "Notably".
- The contrast tic: "It's not just X, it's Y", "This isn't X. It's Y",
  "X isn't the problem. Y is."
- Inflated vocabulary. Use the plain word: delve into → look at; leverage,
  utilize → use; facilitate → help; robust → reliable, or say what makes it
  reliable; seamless → say what the reader does not have to do; comprehensive
  → say what it covers; crucial, pivotal, vital → say the consequence; ensure
  → make sure; surface → show; landscape, realm, tapestry, journey → name the
  thing; underscore, showcase, highlight → show; streamline, holistic,
  nuanced, intricate, elevate, unlock, harness, meticulous, testament → cut.
- Padding by threes: three adjectives or three clauses where one is true.
- Em dashes. Use a full stop, a comma, or brackets.
- Hedge stacks: "may potentially", "could possibly", "generally tends to".
  Keep one hedge where the uncertainty is real, and cut the rest.
- Bold inside a sentence for emphasis, and emoji in headings.
- Idiom and metaphor: "under the hood", "out of the box", "surface area",
  "first-class citizen", unless the subject already uses the term.
- Generic reassurance, hype, and template sections that do not apply.

## Keep exactly as written

- Fenced code blocks and their contents, unchanged and in place.
- Inline code, file paths, line references, commands, identifiers, flags,
  environment variables, URLs, quoted output, and error text.
- Numbers, dates, IDs, ticket keys, PR numbers, model names, and units.
- Terms of art. Explain only local or audience-specific terminology.
- Technical constraints, evidence, caveats, failure modes, security
  implications, and rollback information.
- Every hedge that reflects real uncertainty. State uncertainty as an
  assumption, risk, unknown, or required verification. Do not make the text
  more confident than the evidence, and do not add doubt it does not carry.
